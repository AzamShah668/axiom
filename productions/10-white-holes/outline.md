## Opening (<= 30 s): start in the middle of the action
A NASA satellite (Swift) catches a gamma-ray burst about 1.6 billion light-years away, lasting about 102 seconds. A burst that long should come from a dying star, a supernova. The biggest telescopes looked: no supernova. Some scientists later asked: was it the opposite of a black hole, a white hole, where nothing can go in and everything comes out?

## Ch1 "The Black Hole Played Backwards"
- Context first: what a black hole is (anything goes in, nothing comes out; the falling-ball picture).
- Einstein's equations (1915-16) work the same if time runs backwards: play the video of a black hole in reverse and you get a white hole. Everyday comparison: a video of a ball falling into a well, run backwards.
- Kruskal and Szekeres (1960): the full map of a black hole has a hidden second region, a white hole. Bridge: the maths allowed it, so why did almost nobody believe it?

## Ch2 "Too Strange to Exist"
- Novikov (1964) suggested white holes; Eardley (1974): unstable, a white hole should collapse into a black hole at the smallest disturbance.
- Everyday comparison: a broken glass jumping back together (time-reverse that nature doesn't show).
- **Indian chapter 1:** the same year, Jayant Narlikar, K. M. V. Apparao and Naresh Dadhich (Nature, 1974) argued white holes could explain high-energy radiation in the universe. Bridge: for decades it stayed an idea, until a strange burst in 2006.

## Ch3 "The Burst Without a Star"
- GRB 060614 in detail: long burst, no supernova; Retter and Heller (2011-12) call it a possible white hole, a "Small Bang".
- Honest twist: most astronomers think it was two dead stars merging (later evidence of a kilonova glow). Not proof. Bridge: but quantum gravity gave white holes a new life.

## Ch4 "When a Black Hole Turns White"
- Haggard and Rovelli (2014-15): a black hole could quantum-tunnel into a white hole; for the black hole it is quick, but for us outside it takes billions of years (time slows near it).
- **Indian chapter 2:** Abhay Ashtekar (whose Big Bounce idea closed video 09), with Javier Olmedo and Parampreet Singh (2018): in loop quantum gravity, the black hole's centre has no singularity; it turns into a white hole. Callback to 09.
- Everyday comparison: a ball dropped on the floor bouncing back (careful: 09 used a ball for the bounce, so use a different object, e.g. a squeezed spring).

## Ch5 "Hidden in Plain Sight"
- Bianchi, Rovelli and others (2018): tiny white holes left over from the first black holes could still be around, too small to see; some suggest they could be part of dark matter (hedged).
- Our own past: some physicists note the Big Bang itself looks like a white hole (everything came out, nothing went in), hedged.

## Ending
Debate line (third person): white holes as remnants of black holes, as the Big Bang's twin, or only maths that nature forbids? Science hasn't decided. Callback to the 2006 burst that started the question.
