## 2. What is known about Shorts performance
- View counting changed 2025-03-31: a view counts on start/replay with no minimum watch time; each loop adds a view [C, several blogs agree]. Monetisation uses "engaged views". So raw view counts of 2025+ Shorts are not comparable to older ones.
- Distribution: Short is tested on a small seed audience (~1,000 cited) then expanded if it performs [C, Social Media Examiner]. Watch time/stay-rate is the main optimised signal [C].
- Viewed vs swiped away: share of feed impressions that stayed vs swiped [C, 1of10, Subscribr]. Steep drop in first seconds = weak hook; high viewed but low average % viewed = body is the problem [C]. Benchmarks claimed: 60-80% viewed; no official numbers [C, conflicting].
- Length: quso.ai study of ~108k Shorts: median views highest in 11-20 s, falling after 30 s [C, vendor study]. Adobe Express survey (507 creators): 46% say under 30 s best [C, opinion]. Revid case study (one channel, 220/61/112/108 posts): 30-60 s median 767 views vs <30 s 318, 1-2 min 296 [C, single channel, confounded]. Take-away [E]: sources disagree; the rule that holds is "as short as the idea needs". 25-35 s is fine.
- Loops/replays: widely advised to end where the video began or on a twist; replays 10-20% of views called good [C, no primary source].
- Text on screen: on-screen text hook called "most underutilised" [C, Social Media Examiner]; captions for sound-off viewing [C].
- CTA to long video: sources conflict [C]. Against: "check my full video" ending is a retention killer because viewers want to stay in feed; tacked-on like/subscribe breaks momentum. For: verbal CTA to one specific video plus pinned comment converts better than on-screen text alone. Consensus: deliver payoff first, keep CTA tiny and late (or in pinned comment/related-video link); never before the payoff. No A/B data found. [E] Test it on your own Shorts: one batch with no CTA, one with a 1-second pointer.

## 3. Patterns
(Patterns marked [C] come from guides; the rest are my inference from titles [E].)
- Hook formulas in winners: superlative ("Biggest/Scariest/Most terrifying"), absurd what-if ("needle at light speed"), "Is X still true?" myth-bust, "mystery/secret revealed" (Hindi), "why do everyday space things happen" (astronauts, splashdown). Question hooks work: "Did you know / Have you ever wondered" [C].
- Revid's data: "why/how about everyday space facts" beat grand topics [C, one channel].
- Hook layers: audio line + visual + on-screen text all in the first 1-2 s [C].
- Structure: obstacle/problem then solution; but/therefore chaining keeps loop open [C]. Educational Shorts: end with the promised insight, not a CTA [C].
- Sentence length, visuals per second, "you"/"socho" frequency: NO data found. [E] short spoken sentences (6-10 words), a visual change every 1.5-2.5 s, second person in hook.

## 4. Ten rules for your 25-35 s Hinglish Shorts (rules = my synthesis; evidence tag in brackets)
1. Hook in the first spoken breath, under 2 s, one idea. Bad: "Aaj hum baat karenge black hole ki." Good: "Ek black hole ne ek poora star nigal liya, aur hum use dekh paaye." [C: 1-3 s swipe window]
2. Open with the most extreme fact or a concrete absurd scenario, not the topic name. Superlative or what-if. [V titles]
3. Make the hook a promise with a gap: state the surprise, withhold the "why". "Sooraj ke andar se roshni nikalne mein 1 lakh saal lagte hain. Sirf 8 minute nahi." [C curiosity loop]
4. Put visual + on-screen text hook in frame 0 (big number or 4-word line), matching but not duplicating the audio. [C]
5. Use "tum/aap/socho" once in the first 5 s so the viewer is the subject: "Socho tum Mars par khade ho..." [E]
6. Short sentences, one fact per sentence, drop jargon; Hindi spoken, English only for names/numbers. [E; C says avoid jargon]
7. Escalate: 3 beats (hook, 2 surprising steps, payoff). Each beat ends with a small "but" turn. Payoff by ~75-85% of runtime (about 22-28 s), then stop. [C but/therefore; E timings]
8. A new visual or motion every 1.5-2.5 s; number/number-card moments on the surprise; captions on at all times. [E/C]
9. Loop ending: last line grammatically leads back into the first ("...isliye tum abhi bhi use dekh rahe ho. Ek black hole ne ek star nigal liya." / end on the hook phrase). No "like/subscribe". [C]
10. CTA: none in the body; at most a 1-second "poori kahani neeche" text after the payoff, plus pinned comment with the long video link. Test with/without using your viewed-vs-swiped and subscribers-from-Short stats. [C conflicting; E]

### 5 example hooks (Hinglish, space)
1. "Ek chammach neutron star ka itna bhaari hota hai, ki poore Everest ke barabar." (superlative + absurd scale)
2. "Socho agar Chaand achanak gayab ho jaaye... tum kal subah tak kya mehsoos karoge?" (what-if + you)
3. "NASA ne ek tasveer chhupa ke rakhi thi. Is mein jo hai, wo aaj tak samajh nahi aaya." (mystery with gap)
4. "Astronaut spacecraft se turant bahar kyun nahi aate? Wajah daravni hai." (everyday why, per Revid pattern)
5. "Tumhari aankhon ko jo roshni abhi dikh rahi hai, wo purani hai. Kitni? Sun ke chaunk jaoge." (you + number tease; loop with "kitni purani")
