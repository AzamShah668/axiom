## 2. Openings and movement (8 videos)

Quotes are short; everything else paraphrased. [V = from transcript page summaries]

**A. Kurzgesagt - Moon Crashes into Earth [V]**
- Opening: poses the question, promises the answer is stranger than expected, then asks "why hasn't the Moon already fallen?" Orbit explained via thrown ball. Sets a magic-slowdown premise with a 1-year countdown.
- Movement: month-by-month timeline, each month escalates (tides 4 m -> 10 m -> 30 m+ -> frozen tides -> tides reverse -> crust quakes -> Roche limit -> rings). Aftermath and a sky of rings. Ending: uncertain survival, no moral.
- Technique: countdown structure = built-in open loop ("what's next month?"); the "you" is a survivor.

**B. Kurzgesagt - Black Hole Star [V]**
- Opening: states the thing and its strangeness at once (largest stars that ever existed, brighter than galaxies, with a black hole "parasite" at the core), then a scale number (300 vs up to 10 million solar masses). Promise: it solves a big cosmology puzzle.
- Movement: rules of ordinary stars -> early universe lets those rules break -> birth -> black hole core -> violent end -> payoff: explains early supermassive black holes. Ending hands off to JWST observation. No explicit CTA in transcript.
- Technique: "rule, then break the rule"; the puzzle is named in the first 30 s and answered at ~8:00.

**C. Kurzgesagt - Deadliest Virus (rabies) [V]**
- Opening: cold open with an odd image (1970s planes dropping vaccine baits over Europe), then the name's Greek origin and "only five genes" paradox.
- Movement: bite -> nerve hijack -> immune blindness -> "Uno reverse" on T cells -> death -> TURN: vaccination works because the virus is slow early -> ending with 60,000 deaths/year and a hopeful "we can defeat it".
- Technique: villain narrative with a human-scale stake; the "but" turn arrives ~70% in.

**D. Veritasium - Big Misconception About Electricity [V]**
- Opening: thought experiment (battery, switch, bulb 1 m away, two wires each ~300,000 km long); how long until the bulb lights? Viewers are asked to commit to an answer in comments before the reveal.
- Movement: the wrong story (electrons carry energy) -> field/Poynting explanation -> AC -> undersea telegraph cable history -> the answer (~1/c s) near the end; sponsor segment last.
- Technique: make the viewer guess first (misconception-first, per Muller's research, see section 3). The answer is withheld nearly the whole video; the long middle explains the machinery.

**E. Veritasium - Most Misunderstood Concept in Physics [V]**
- Opening: enormous promise (molecular collisions to fate of the universe, maybe the direction of time), then street-interview question "what does Earth get from the Sun?"; the answer "energy" is a misconception (in roughly equals out).
- Movement: history (Carnot) -> ideal engine -> entropy reframed from "disorder" to "energy spreading" -> AC paradox -> life on Earth -> past hypothesis -> black holes -> heat death -> tea-and-milk ending. Several mini misconception turns about every 3-5 min. Sponsor after conclusion.
- Technique: each chapter flips something the viewer believed.

**F. Cleo Abram - What If You Just Keep Digging? [V]**
- Opening: real record (Soviet Kola borehole, deeper than the ocean trench) then the hypothetical, with "you" as the digger; temperature rising at ~0:54.
- Movement: descent by layers; "how do we know?" detective detour (seismic waves); sponsor interlude mid-descent; ends on unknowns (2% of the way) and "curious species" note.
- Technique: real-world anchor then thought experiment; stakes at personal ("crushed and crispy"), planetary (magnetic field) and human (moonshot) scale.

**G. Vsauce - Which Way Is Down? [V]**
- Opening: playful word-question ("what is down, how much does it weigh") then a concrete simulation (Sun to black hole in Universe Sandbox).
- Movement: 5 open loops seeded early, answered in order via 6 turns (shrunken Sun changes nothing -> mass vs weight -> down is messy -> equivalence -> geodesics -> spacetime). Ending: "down" points where time runs slowest; ties back to the opening question.
- Technique: question ladder; every answer reveals a deeper question.

**H. Mark Rober - Egg Drop From Space [V]**
- Opening: simple record challenge, escalation to space, a self-warning ("most draining video I would ever attempt").
- Movement: tests, failures, pivot (consult NASA engineer), escalation, last-minute problems, payoff, lesson about failure, own product plug last.
- Technique: failure beats are the retention; stakes = three years of effort.

**I. melodysheep - Timelapse of the Future [V, transcript only]**
- Opening: a question, a quote, a premise ("playback speed doubles every five seconds"); a formal device sets the pacing. Slow sections where explanation is needed; ends quiet (last light, cold dark).

**J. Sabine - Why does light exist? [V]**
- Opening: one-line question and an immediate bold answer claim ("Light exists because electric charges exist"); then steps. Sponsor near end; sign-off, no subscribe ask.

## 3. What creators say (with confidence labels)

**Derek Muller / Veritasium**
- Doctoral research (Univ. Sydney 2008, "Designing Effective Multimedia for Physics Education") found clear expository videos taught little; a video that voiced common misconceptions did better (reported pre 6.0 / post 6.3 for plain explanation vs 11/26 with misconceptions). [S, via thejournal.com / emergingedtech / summaries; study numbers not checked against thesis]
- Scientific American profile: "Some level of discomfort seems to be essential"; since 2012's watch-time shift he makes longer videos "unspooling each episode like a mystery novel" with layered demonstrations and chained mysteries. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-youtube-star-derek-muller-of-veritasium-is-challenging-scientific/ [V]
- Interview piece (creatorhandbook.net): pose a puzzle first, surface wrong beliefs, show why they fail, then explain; "engage with people's prior knowledge". [V of that page, which is a re-post]
- "My Video Went Viral. Here's Why" (2019): viral = high click-through x high watch time; titles/thumbnails are the hook; ~15 min videos to reach 7-8 min average watch; "The audience only knows you're there if they see your titles and thumbnails." https://rosetta.to/u/veritasium/my-video-went-viral-here-s-why [V]
- Summaries (AI-made) credit him with "double barrel" title+thumbnail and asking a question to make viewers "hungry" before the explanation. [S]

**Kurzgesagt**
- Official process: books/primary papers -> expert consultation -> source sheet; script "can take about a dozen drafts"; illustration -> narration sets animation timing -> custom music. https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/ [V]
- Head of Research / AMA (via mirrors): topics are scrapped if facts are not "surprising or fresh enough"; scripts are fact-checked sentence by sentence; Philipp Dettmer: ~60% of his week is script and research; a script that won't fit the ~10-minute format is a problem. [S, mirror sites]
- Typical figures "1,200 hours per video" are secondary. [S]

**Cleo Abram**
- Plans visuals before writing (a visual abstract); topic filters: inherently visual + "huge if true" potential impact; episode shape = how it works, why it's fascinating, implications. [S, interview summaries on podwise etc.]

**MrBeast-style retention applied to education**
- Mario Joos (ex-MrBeast strategist), via Vidpros write-up: do not chase 70%; 100M+ videos average around 40%; slow pacing comes from non-progressive scenes, not cut speed; hook is a promise of something better; payoffs must exceed the expectation; title cards help viewers orient; in educational videos lead with new or surprising information; lower, personal "narrator mode". https://vidpros.com/insiders/mario-joos-youtube-retention-secrets/ [S]
- Generic: first 30 s decide the most drop-off; open loops are hinted and closed later, not all at once. [S, blogs]
- Paddy Galloway: I found only third-party summaries (five-act-style retention framework, don't overreact to micro dips). Nothing specific to science. [S, weak]

## 4. Patterns

**Hook types**
1. Strange real image/event (rabies bait planes, Kola borehole) -> then the question.
2. Bold puzzle the viewer is asked to answer (electricity wires, "what does Earth get from the Sun?").
3. Superlative + paradox (largest star ever, with a black hole inside; "stars that shouldn't exist").
4. Hypothetical with a clock (Moon, 12 months).
5. Challenge/record (egg from space).
Rule: the first 30 s state the thing, the strangeness, and the payoff to come; no channel opens with a greeting or channel intro. [V across the transcripts]

**How the promise is set**: "this will explain X" plus a reason it matters (solves cosmology puzzle; explains the arrow of time). Veritasium promises more than the video can obviously carry, then delivers by chapters. Promise is usually restated by a scale number or question within 30 s.

**Open loops**: Vsauce plants 4-5 questions in the first minutes; Veritasium holds the answer to the headline puzzle until the last third; Kurzgesagt uses a timeline or physical sequence so "what's next" is automatic.

**The "but" turn**: ordinary rule -> exception (black hole star); myth -> truth (misconception chapters); disaster -> hope (rabies vaccine at ~70%). Veritasium entropy video has a turn about every 3-5 min. [V]

**Pacing of reveals**: each 1-3 min block ends with a new fact that changes the previous one (Kurzgesagt) or a reframed definition (Veritasium). Bigger payoff around 70-85%; a quieter "what remains unknown" close. [E from outlines]

**Use of "you"**: Cleo makes the viewer the digger; Kurzgesagt makes you a survivor of the Moon crash; Veritasium makes you guess; Vsauce asks you the child's question. Third-person documentary voice appears in melodysheep. [V]

**Stakes**: personal (you'd be crushed), planetary (magnetic field, civilization), human (60,000 deaths/year), existential (heat death), effort (three years). At least two scales per video. [V]

**Analogies**: ball thrown so fast it circles the globe; tea and milk; "Uno reverse"; ribbon on a sphere. Concrete everyday object, then the number. [V]

**Length**: 9-12 min (Kurzgesagt), 11-20 (Cleo), 25-35 (Veritasium/Vsauce long-form). Muller: aim so average view time is 7-8 min -> ~15 min. [V/S] Short is not the cause of virality, topic and packaging are. [E]

**Endings**: Kurzgesagt ends on a reflective or hopeful line or an open question, rarely a CTA in the transcript; Veritasium and Cleo end with a thesis line then sponsor/thanks; Rober ends with a lesson then product plug; Vsauce ties back to the opening question. Next-video handoffs were minimal in these transcripts (I saw no "watch this next" lines; end-screen cards cannot be seen from text). [V on transcripts; gap on end screens]

**Sponsor placement**: after the hook and first chapter (Rober's maze/Cleo's mid-descent) or at the very end (Veritasium entropy, Sabine). Never before the promise is set. [V]

## 5. Ten script-writing rules (with examples for Hindi space)

1. **Open with the strange thing, not the topic.** First 3 lines: image/fact + paradox + what you'll learn. Ex: "1977 mein ek aisa signal aaya ki aaj tak koi samjha nahi paya" instead of "aaj hum Wow signal ke baare mein baat karenge."
2. **Name the promise in the first 30 s and attach a number.** Ex: "Ek tara jo Sooraj se 10 lakh guna bada tha... aur jiske andar ek black hole baitha tha."
3. **Plant 3-5 open loops in the first two minutes, close them in order** (Vsauce), and keep one big loop open until ~70-85% (Veritasium).
4. **Make the viewer guess a plausible wrong answer before you correct it.** Ex: "Aapko kya lagta hai, Chand dharti par gir jaye toh sabse pehle kya hoga?" Then the real order is surprising.
5. **Use a clock or a staircase structure** (Moon: month 1 -> 12; digging: crust -> core; Timelapse: doubling years). Built-in "what's next".
6. **Give every 1-3 minute block a turn: "lekin..."** The new fact should overturn or deepen the last one. Audit: if a block only adds information, cut or merge it.
7. **Anchor in a real event, then go hypothetical** (Cleo's Kola hole) so "what if" feels grounded.
8. **Put the viewer inside the story** ("aap" as digger, survivor, falling astronaut) and use at least two scales of stakes (personal + planetary).
9. **Explain with one everyday analogy per concept and cut anything non-progressive** (Joos: slowness is stacked non-progressive scenes, not slow cuts). One idea per chapter, titled on screen.
10. **End by echoing the opening and giving the next hook.** Close the first question, admit the open unknown, one line of awe or hope, then a specific next-video question tied to the unanswered piece. (Ending handoff is my [E] adaptation, English channels here mostly end with a reflection or sponsor.)

Adaptation notes for Hindi [E]
- Kurzgesagt-length (9-12 min, ~1,500-1,900 spoken Hindi words at normal pace) is the easiest model to copy; Veritasium-length needs a real mystery and visuals/demonstrations you cannot fake with stock footage.
- Do not copy: Veritasium street-interview device, Rober builds, Vsauce stream-of-association. Copy the mechanics: promise, misconception, loop, turn.
- Attribute facts yourself (Kurzgesagt-style source sheet) and hedge physics claims.
